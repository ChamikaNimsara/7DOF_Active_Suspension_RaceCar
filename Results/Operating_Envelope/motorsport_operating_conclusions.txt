MOTORSPORT OPERATING-CONDITION CONCLUSIONS
==========================================

The final tuned LQR controller remained well within the defined 3000 N actuator-force limit throughout the evaluated operating conditions.

At the 144 km/h asymmetric kerb condition, maximum actuator utilization was 21.87 %, with a peak suspension deflection of 20.298 mm and peak relative suspension velocity of 2.668 m/s.

Increasing vehicle speed from 72 km/h to 144 km/h for the fixed-length kerb reduced peak heave by 38.24 %, peak pitch by 48.25 %, peak roll by 44.56 % and peak actuator force by 40.85 %.

This reduction in sprung-mass response should not be interpreted as the high-speed kerb being less mechanically severe. The shorter road-input duration produced high suspension velocities and strong wheel-level transient behaviour.

The linear tyre model predicted a minimum normal load of -6603.44 N and temporary unloading for up to 0.2599 % of the simulation. Negative predicted tyre loads are non-physical and indicate that the linear tyre model has exceeded its valid operating region.

For future high-fidelity kerb-impact analysis, the model should therefore be extended with unilateral tyre contact, nonlinear tyre stiffness and potentially nonlinear damper behaviour.

Within the valid linear operating region, the tuned LQR controller demonstrates effective control of sprung-mass motion while requiring substantially less than the available actuator-force capability.
